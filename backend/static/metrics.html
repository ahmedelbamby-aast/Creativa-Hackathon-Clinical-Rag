Foundational metrics · plain-English guide

What do the quality numbers mean?

These metrics help us see whether search found the right evidence, whether an answer matched a known reference, and whether the system stayed fast, reliable, and affordable.

ImportantNot measured is different from zero. It means that chat has no reviewed gold answer or relevance label, so the system does not invent a score.

Search quality

Did retrieval find the evidence?

These scores need reviewed labels that say which document or passage is relevant.

01

Hit Rate@k

Higher is better

How often at least one relevant result appears in the first k results.

Role
Basic search success check.
Use
Compare embeddings, chunk sizes, or search settings.
Example: In 8 of 10 questions, a useful passage is in the top 5. Hit Rate@5 = 0.80.
02

Precision@k

Higher is better

The share of the first k results that are relevant.

Role
Shows how focused the retrieved context is.
Use
Reduce noisy or distracting passages.
Example: 3 of the top 5 passages are useful. Precision@5 = 3 ÷ 5 = 0.60.
03

Recall@k

Higher is better

The share of all known relevant evidence found in the first k results.

Role
Shows whether important evidence was missed.
Use
Important for questions that need several facts or sources.
Example: The gold set has 4 useful passages and search finds 3. Recall@5 = 3 ÷ 4 = 0.75.
04

Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR)

Higher is better

How early the first relevant result appears, averaged across questions.

Role
Rewards putting the first useful passage near the top.
Use
Best when one passage can answer the question.
Example: The first useful result is rank 2. Reciprocal rank = 1 ÷ 2 = 0.50.
05

Mean Average Precision (MAP)

Higher is better

Measures whether all relevant results are placed early, then averages the score across questions.

Role
Checks the quality of the full ranked list.
Use
Use when several passages may be relevant.
Example: Relevant passages at ranks 1 and 3 score better than the same passages at ranks 4 and 5.
06

nDCG@k

Higher is better

Rewards highly relevant evidence near the top and can give partial credit to partly useful evidence.

Role
Combines relevance strength and ranking position.
Use
Useful when evidence has grades such as full, partial, or irrelevant.
Example: A fully supporting passage at rank 1 scores better than a partly related passage at rank 1.
Answer quality

Did the final answer match the target?

These scores are only meaningful for chats with a reviewed reference answer or a clear pass rule.

07

Exact Match (EM)

Higher is better

Whether the normalized answer exactly equals an accepted reference answer.

Role
Strict check for short facts, dates, names, or IDs.
Use
Use for answers with one clear form.
Example: Reference: “589 million.” Answer: “589 million.” EM = 1. A longer explanation may score 0 even if correct.
08

Token Precision, Recall, and F1

Higher is better

Compares the words in the answer with the words in the reference. F1 balances precision and recall.

Role
Gives partial credit when wording is not identical.
Use
Pair with Exact Match for short-answer questions.
Example: Reference: "589 million adults." Answer: "589 million." Precision is high, recall is lower, and F1 balances both.
09

End-to-End Task Success

Higher is better

Whether the complete system passed a rule defined before testing.

Role
Answers the simple question: did the whole task work?
Use
Track business or safety outcomes across releases.
Example: A labeled unsupported question is safely rejected without calling a model. The defined task passes, so success = 1.
10

Faithfulness

Higher is better

The fraction of claims in the generated answer that are supported by the retrieved evidence context.

Role
Central hallucination-control metric — detects when the model generates information not present in the supplied evidence.
Use
Run at claim level; inspect unsupported claims for debugging. Pair with Task Success and Token F1. A high score means the answer stays grounded in the retrieved passages.
Example: The answer contains 4 claims. 3 are found in the retrieved evidence. Faithfulness = 3 ÷ 4 = 0.75. The unsupported claim may indicate hallucination.
Operations

Was the system fast, reliable, and efficient?

These metrics are available for every completed chat, even when no gold answer exists.

11

Latency Percentiles

Lower is better

How long retrieval, generation, and the full request take. We show p50, p95, and p99.

Role
Shows normal speed and slow "tail" requests.
Use
Find slow stages and compare releases fairly.
Example: p95 = 4 seconds means 95% of requests finish in 4 seconds or less.
12

Error Rate and Availability

Errors ↓ Availability ↑

Error rate is failed requests divided by eligible requests. Availability is the successful share.

Role
Separates service failures from answer-quality problems.
Use
Track database, retrieval, provider, timeout, and HTTP failures.
Example: 98 of 100 eligible requests succeed. Availability = 98%; error rate = 2%.
13

Token Usage and Cost per Request

Lower at equal quality

Counts input, retrieved-context, and output tokens, then estimates provider cost when pricing is configured.

Role
Shows resource use and oversized context.
Use
Compare models or prompts only at similar quality.
Example: A request uses 2,000 input tokens and 300 output tokens. Its cost uses the configured price for each token type.
